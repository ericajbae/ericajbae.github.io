Research

Where scientific curiosity begins

Four studies, one question: what in a young child's mind and surroundings predicts who will still be engaged with science ten years later?

Ongoing projects

01

Exploring the Origins of Scientific Literacy

This longitudinal study tracks children from ages 3 to 12 to examine how early individual differences in interests, causal reasoning skills, and home environment shape the development of scientific literacy and engagement.

NSF Awards #2300024, #1535102 · PI: Amy E. Booth

02

Measuring Early Motivations in Science

This project examines the psychometric properties of novel measures designed to assess early scientific interest in preschoolers.

Developed as part of my qualifying examination; this work serves as the foundation for my ongoing doctoral dissertation.

03

Examining the Role of Home and Parents in Emergent Scientific Motivation

This study examines how components of the Home Science Environment (HSE) are associated with preschool-aged children's scientific interest. By looking at science-related home resources, engagements, and the underlying attitudinal climate in which those are embedded, this study aims to better identify the dimensions of the home science environment that explain young children's emerging scientific interest.

Part of my ongoing doctoral dissertation.

Completed projects

Preschool Science Show Study

This study investigated the impact of live science workshops on preschoolers' scientific interest and knowledge, in collaboration with a local Nashville vendor (Mr. Bond's Science Guys) and preschool (Fannie Battle Day Home). Recognizing the need for better tools to assess early science interest, this project directly inspired my measurement study.

PI: Dr. Amy E. Booth

AI-Driven Shared Book Reading Mobile Application

This study evaluated the usability and impact of a mobile app that uses AI-generated questions for parent–child dialogic reading.

PI: Dr. Margaret Shavlik
